Why Coiba

The Galápagos you can reach by lunchtime.

Coiba was a penal colony for almost a century, which kept its forests and reefs untouched while the rest of the Pacific coast was developed. Today it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the largest island in Central America, protected along with 38 smaller islands and the water around them.

The same currents that feed the Galápagos pass here. Volcanic pinnacles rise from deep water and pull in pelagics: whale sharks in the dry season, humpbacks in the green season, hammerheads off Contreras, and whitetip reef sharks on nearly every dive.

When to come

Coiba dives all year. What you see changes.

The dry season brings cool, nutrient-rich upwellings and the big filter feeders. The green season brings warm water, flat seas, humpbacks and the best visibility.

Before you book

Questions we answer every morning.

Do I need to be certified to dive Coiba?

For the daily trips, yes: Open Water or equivalent. If you have never dived, book Discover Scuba Diving and you will do two real dives in the park with an instructor holding your hand. Advanced Open Water is recommended for the deeper sites at Contreras and Canales.

What is the park fee and why is it not included?

Coiba National Park charges every visitor an entrance fee: $20 per day for foreigners, $5 for Panamanians and residents. It is collected by the park rangers in cash, so we cannot include it in online payments. Bring exact change if you can.

How do I get to Santa Catalina?

From Panama City, drive about 5 to 6 hours via Santiago and Soná, or take the bus to Soná and a local bus or taxi from there. Albrook terminal has buses to Santiago all day. We can arrange a private transfer from Panama City or David.

What should I bring?

Certification card, swimwear, towel, sunscreen, a hat, and cash for the park fee. We provide all dive equipment, including wetsuits. In the dry season a 5 mm suit is included because thermoclines can drop to 20 °C.

Can non-divers come on the boat?

Yes. Snorkellers and companions can join the trip at a reduced rate, snorkel the surface intervals at Granito de Oro, and spend the day on the island while the group dives.

How far ahead should I book?

In the dry season, December to April, boats fill a week or more ahead. The rest of the year a few days is usually enough. Courses need a start date agreed in advance so we can schedule an instructor.

Do you fill Nitrox?

Yes, on request and with a Nitrox certification. Ask when booking so tanks are ready the night before.

What is your cancellation policy?

Trips cancelled by us for weather or sea state are refunded or rebooked in full. Guest cancellations more than 48 hours ahead are refunded minus processing. Within 48 hours we do our best to rebook you.
